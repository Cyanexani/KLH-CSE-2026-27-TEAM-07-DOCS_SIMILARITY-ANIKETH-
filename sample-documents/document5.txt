Study Notes: Inventions That Changed Communication

1. The printing press. Around 1440, Johannes Gutenberg, a goldsmith from the German city of Mainz, began to develop a new way of printing. His key idea was movable metal type. By the year 1500, printing shops were operating in more than two hundred cities, and millions of books had been produced.

2. The telegraph. In the 1830s and 1840s, electric telegraph systems made it possible to send coded messages along wires almost instantly. Samuel Morse and Alfred Vail developed the code of dots and dashes that became the standard for decades.

3. The telephone. In 1876 Alexander Graham Bell received a patent for a device that carried the human voice as an electrical signal. Within a few decades, telephone lines connected homes and offices in most large cities.

4. Radio. Early in the twentieth century, wireless transmission allowed news, music and emergency messages to reach listeners far from any cable.
