Renewable energy sources such as solar, wind, and hydroelectric power are becoming increasingly important as the world seeks to reduce its reliance on fossil fuels. Solar panels convert sunlight directly into electricity through the photovoltaic effect, while wind turbines harness kinetic energy from moving air masses. Hydroelectric dams generate power by channeling the force of flowing water through turbines connected to generators.

The transition to renewable energy is driven by both environmental concerns and economic factors. As the cost of solar panels and wind turbines continues to fall, renewable energy has become competitive with, and in many cases cheaper than, traditional fossil fuel generation. Governments around the world have introduced incentives and subsidies to accelerate this transition, recognizing the long-term benefits of a cleaner energy grid.

Energy storage remains one of the biggest challenges facing renewable energy adoption. Because solar and wind power generation depends on weather conditions, grid operators need reliable ways to store excess energy for use during periods of low generation. Battery technology, pumped hydro storage, and emerging solutions like green hydrogen are all being explored as ways to solve this intermittency problem.
